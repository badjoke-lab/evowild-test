## KEEP from v1

- The old thin four-strip comb interpretation is gone.
- Two dominant crest blades are now the primary silhouette.
- FRONT and BACK clearly show two separated dominant blades. This is directionally correct and must not be "fixed" by collapsing them into one centerline crest.
- The head/neck posture moved forward and lower relative to the rejected B2a baseline.
- R0-A1 scope control passed: topology and all geometry outside the head/neck support remained fixed.

## REVISE — head

The SIDE wedge is too needle-like and pinched at the snout. The authority head is long and sharp, but retains a broader faceted skull/snout volume and a more integrated cranial wedge.

R0-A1 v2 must preserve forward length while retaining more lateral width at the forward head.

## REVISE — dominant crest

The SIDE trajectory is too steep/high. v1 rises into a tall sail above the skull; the authority blades are extremely long but sweep much farther backward relative to their vertical rise.

FRONT/BACK also remain too narrow and straight, reading as tall ear-like strips. The authority front/back blades have broader faceted/diamond plate surfaces while remaining a clearly separated pair.

R0-A1 v2 must:
- lower the dominant control/tip Z;
- extend the sweep farther rearward;
- increase visible broad-face projection in FRONT/BACK;
- preserve two clearly separated blades;
- keep SIDE blades broad and layered, not thin rods.

## REVISE — secondary crest

The secondary layers are directionally useful but follow the v1 crest too vertically. They must be lowered into the same shallower rearward stack and remain subordinate to the two dominant blades.

## REVISE — neck

v1 still reads as a smooth swollen/tubular neck through the middle. The authority uses a more angular tapered neck: relatively compact near the head, then a controlled flare/depth increase toward the shoulder root.

R0-A1 v2 must remove the mid-neck sinusoidal bulge. The shoulder boundary itself remains hard-fixed; no R0-A2 shoulder/chest remassing is permitted yet.
